# 30-Customer Dogfood Review

Date: 2026-04-24

This is a synthetic product dogfood pass, not real customer research. I treated the product as if 30 domain users were trying to solve a live post-inspection deal problem and asked one question: would this artifact help them act faster, with less deal risk, today?

## Version Context

This review explains why v2 should exist.

v1 was the broad repair-cost/pSEO version. It failed because broad discovery did not create a clear buyer-agent job or fast-money path. The 30-customer review supports v2 because the strongest users all cluster around one urgent moment: a live post-inspection ask that needs to be checked before it is sent.

## Product Judgment

The product is strongest when the user is a buyer agent, team lead, or transaction coordinator with a live financed file, an inspection deadline, and a proposed ask that may be too broad. In that situation the useful output is not "content." It is a defensibility check: what to keep, what to cut, what evidence is missing, whether credit or repair is cleaner, and whether the packet is safe to send.

The product is weaker for broad buyers researching repairs, sellers defending a listing, investors building a rehab budget, and inspectors who already have request-list software. Those users may still get value, but they are not the fastest-money ICP.

Synthetic fit estimate from the 30-case set:

- Strong "would use now": 17 / 30
- Useful as review/checklist but not a final decision: 9 / 30
- Weak or wrong first customer: 4 / 30

## Business Viability Review

Business-persona verdict: this topic can make money, but not as a broad consumer blog and not as a one-off "repair cost calculator." The viable business is a narrow workflow product for buyer agents, small buyer-agent teams, and transaction coordinators who repeatedly handle post-inspection requests.

Why the business case exists:

- The transaction moment is expensive. A bad inspection ask can lose a concession, delay loan documents, weaken leverage, or cause a buyer to walk.
- The buyer-agent workflow is repeatable. One buyer may use the tool once, but agents and coordinators can use it on many files.
- Current SERP competitors mostly explain negotiation options. Few behave like a decision tool that checks the actual ask, evidence, fallback, lender risk, and form/deadline gates.
- New-domain trust is a real constraint, so monetization should follow proof signals: packet generation, copy/print, repeated same-user use, and buyer-agent setup requests.

What the business is not:

- Not a generalized home repair SEO site.
- Not a substitute for an agent, lender, attorney, inspector, contractor, or state form.
- Not a pricing oracle. Numbers should stay labeled as quote-backed, estimate-only, evidence-backed, lender-confirmation needed, or do-not-send.

The fastest-money path is free tool usage now, then a paid buyer-agent/team workflow only after repeat-use signals appear. The offer should eventually be "make inspection responses safer and faster across live files," not "read more about home inspection repairs."

## Persona Consensus

30 synthetic users: strongest pull came from buyer agents, team leads, and transaction coordinators with a live financed file. Weakest pull came from broad homeowners, sellers, investors, and inspectors.

10 genius product personas: the core product should be a decision-compression tool. The output must answer "send, revise, or do not send" and explain why.

10 business personas: the topic is monetizable if sold as workflow risk reduction for repeat deal handlers. They would not monetize consumers first; they would watch for agent/team setup intent.

10 SERP/SEO personas: the wedge is viable only if each page has a distinct transaction job and sample case. Forty pages that merely paraphrase the same article create doorway/scaled-content risk.

## What I Would Change For Acquisition

Keep one product identity: `Inspection Ask Pre-Send Check`.

Expand only through transaction-decision surfaces:

- Keep the first 7 core surfaces around response letter, credit, repair, objection, first ask, repair-vs-credit, and deadline.
- Expand to 40 total surfaces only where the visitor has a live transaction decision: counter/refusal, scope/reasonableness, financing, form/deadline, or a high-stakes defect category.
- Keep proof/commercial support: `/for-buyer-agents`, `/sample-seller-credit-request-after-home-inspection`, `/fha-va-inspection-repairs-and-seller-credit`

Do not go back to mass city/era/component pSEO. That was the original failure mode: broad surface, weak identity, low click intent. The new expansion should look like different doorways into the same tool, but not doorway pages that only exist to funnel users. Each surface needs a distinct decision job.

## What I Would Remove From Legacy

Retire unrelated legacy repair-cost pages with `410 Gone` plus `noindex`. Do not mass-redirect every old URL to `/home-repair`, because that tells users and Google the new product is a generic repair-cost replacement.

Use redirects only when the old URL is truly close to the new job:

- old buyer-intent planner entry -> `/home-repair?legacy=planner`
- old malformed or repeated extension URLs -> `410 Gone`
- old city/era/component verdict pages -> no sitemap inclusion and hard retirement unless a specific URL has real inspection-negotiation demand

This matches Google Search Central guidance: redirects and canonicals can consolidate duplicate or very similar URLs, while scaled pages or doorway-style pages that add little user value are a risk surface.

## Remaining Risk

The biggest business risk is still trust, not UI. A new domain can look polished while giving a number that feels made up. The product should keep proving itself through conservative labels: estimate-only, quote-backed, evidence-backed, lender-confirmation needed, form-review needed, and do-not-send when gates fail.

Next proof target: use real inspection report examples and measure whether users copy/print the packet. Monetization should wait until copied/printed packets or agent setup requests appear.
